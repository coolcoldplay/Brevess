Lactylation-driven therapeutic resistance in cancer: Mechanisms and therapeutic opportunities

Wanghao Zhang, Guanglong Huang, Woheng Tang, Jingxian Chen, Yaojiang Feng, Kaichen Li

Abstract

Protein lactylation connects tumor metabolic reprogramming with epigenetic regulation and therapeutic resistance. This review integrates recent advances in lactylation writers, readers, and erasers and evaluates translational opportunities.

Overview of lactylation

The discovery of lactylation

Lactate is no longer considered merely a metabolic waste product. It acts as a signaling substrate that reshapes transcription, chromatin state, and cellular adaptation under stress.

Increased lactylation was associated with sustained tumor-cell survival and resistance across several experimental systems. These findings define a plausible connection between metabolic state and treatment response.

Cellular adaptation and resistance

Hypoxia-associated metabolic change supports cellular plasticity. Nutrient deprivation can maintain mitochondrial respiration and enable survival during chemotherapy or radiotherapy.

The acidic tumor microenvironment may reduce drug permeability and support immune evasion. This sentence has been manually highlighted; select it to preview category and note editing in the side panel.

Mechanisms of therapeutic resistance

Histone and non-histone lactylation can stabilize stemness programs, remodel macrophage states, and sustain compensatory signaling. However, many reported relationships remain associative rather than causal.

Therapeutic strategies

Candidate strategies include targeting lactate production, blocking transporters, modulating lactylation enzymes, and combining metabolic interventions with immunotherapy.

Conclusions and perspectives

The field requires stronger causal experiments, external validation, dose–response evidence, and clearer separation of preclinical promise from demonstrated clinical benefit.
